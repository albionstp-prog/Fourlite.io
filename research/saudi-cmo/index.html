      IN PLACE
      Right in force since —, for communication to the public "by any means" (Art. 11). Radio, TV, hotels, venues, events, and probably online radio.
      MISSING
      No published tariff or rate-setting mechanism yet. Pricing is negotiable until one exists.
      DRAFT
      SAIP published draft licensing rules in March 2026: a 3-year licence, admin costs capped at 25%, external audit. Its board discussed them on 28 September 2026. Collecting bodies licensed: —.
      NO DEFAULT
      By agreement only (Art. 11). No default share and no fallback, and a contract can probably move the performer's share (Art. 20).
      IN PLACE
      Performer and label claim jointly unless they agree otherwise (Art. 11(2)). Performers in a group must agree who represents them (Art. 9(3)).

        FAQ

        common questions.

        Who will collect, and when?

        SAIP published a draft licensing regulation in March 2026 to prepare the first Saudi collecting body: a 3-year licence, membership as a mandate rather than a transfer, admin costs capped at 25%, and an external audit. A law-firm review on 27 August said the framework was not yet in place. SAIP's board discussed the draft on 28 September; no approval or licensed body had been announced by 16 November. Who will run it is not known. C

        How is the money split between performer and label?

        One shared remuneration. Article 11 lets the two sides agree who claims and how it is divided, and sets no default and no fallback. C Article 20 makes economic rights transferable, so a contract can probably move the performer's share. The collecting body's distribution rules may add a default, but none has been published. CE

        Our catalogue is with a major. Aren't we covered?

        Possibly for the label's half. Producer mandates usually don't claim the performers' side, and a distribution deal may not include neighbouring rights at all. In Saudi Arabia the claim is joint unless performer and label agree otherwise (Art. 11(2)), so a label-only mandate may not be enough without a performer agreement. Check both. CE

        Is online radio covered?

        Probably. Article 11 covers communication to the public "by any means", which plausibly includes simulcast and non-interactive webcasting. On-demand streaming is licensed directly under the exclusive making-available right, not through the collecting body. Untested until the first tariffs. CE

        Will Saudi recordings be paid abroad, and foreign recordings here?

        Here: Article 2(2) treats nationals of any copyright or neighbouring-rights treaty the Kingdom has joined (Berne, TRIPS, the Phonograms Convention) like Saudi nationals, and the Royal Decree limits this where a country does not protect Saudis. C Abroad: each country applies its own rules. Saudi Arabia is outside Rome and WPPT, so a Saudi label's recording usually qualifies only through where it was first recorded or released, country by country. A recording made and released only in Saudi Arabia can miss out in France altogether. CE

        Do we need to register with SAIP?

        Registration is documentary and gives a rebuttable presumption of ownership. Where it opens for recordings, attach each holder's percentage (Arts. 45, 49(2), 61). C

        How much will collection cost?

        The draft caps admin deductions at 25% of collections. For comparison, Greece's licensing front GEA spent 22.4% of collections in 2024 (19.4% on its own measure, before the member societies' own costs), and South Africa's SAMPRA 19.9%. C

        Who has to pay?

        Only commercial use. Article 11(4) limits the right to commercial broadcast or communication; private and non-commercial uses and the statutory exceptions are out. C

        Can we collect anything before a Saudi body exists?

        For Saudi usage, no. But Saudi rights holders can already mandate EMRA or Music Nation for UAE income, which is collected from 1 December 2026, and foreign societies or agents for income abroad where their recordings qualify. CE

          About the monitor

          why this exists.

          Why

          Saudi Arabia is building collective management from scratch. The first distributions will reward whoever's data and contracts are ready. This tracker helps Saudi rights holders act before the statements arrive, not reconcile after.

          How

          The gazetted regulations, the Royal Decree, law-firm analysis, official statistics and published collecting-body reports. Every claim is graded C confirmed, CE estimate or D directional. No CMO data.

          How to read it

          • C confirmed: stated in the instrument or a named official, legal or published source.
          • CE calibrated estimate: calculated or inferred, with the working shown.
          • D directional: a single secondary source, or not yet verified.
          • Orange is Saudi Arabia. Violet is the UAE and other comparison markets.

          Methodology

          • Primary text: the Royal Decree and the gazetted Implementing Regulations.
          • Draft CMO regulation as reported in Arabic press; law-firm analysis from Gowling WLG, Baker McKenzie, Al Tamimi.
          • Market estimate: UAE all-music tariff bands applied to MBC's BOCA segment and the licensed hotel count, then 50% taken as the recordings share (assumption). Comparable bodies' collections per person × Saudi population as a cross-check.

          Honesty

          • No estimate is presented as a fact.
          • The market estimate is the level once licensing is in place at UAE-style rates, not a year-one forecast.
          • No streaming data is used to size public performance.
          • No CMO data. Independent of any collecting body.

          Limitations

          • Turkey's figure is producer distributions only; Morocco publishes none.
          • Portugal's figure may cover producers only.
          • Draft CMO regulation: SAIP's board discussed it on 28 Sep 2026; nothing later found by 16 Nov.
          • The UAE split between songs and recordings is unpublished, so the 50% recordings share is an assumption.
          • UAE rates are single-source (Al Tamimi).
          • Term of protection unconfirmed.
