FAQ

questions labels ask.

Who will collect, and when?

SAIP published a draft licensing regulation in March 2026 to prepare the first Saudi collecting body: a 3-year licence, membership as a mandate rather than a transfer, admin costs capped at 25%, and an external audit. A law-firm review on 27 August said the framework was not yet in place. Who will run it has not been announced. C

How is the money split between performer and label?

One shared remuneration. Article 11 lets the two sides agree who claims and how it is divided, and sets no default and no fallback. Article 20 lets a contract transfer the performer's share. The collecting body's distribution rules may add a default, but none has been published. C

Our catalogue is with a major. Aren't we covered?

Possibly for the label's half. Producer mandates usually don't claim the performers' side, and a distribution deal may not include neighbouring rights at all. Check both. CE

Is online radio covered?

Probably. Article 11 covers communication to the public "by any means", which plausibly includes simulcast and non-interactive webcasting. On-demand streaming is licensed directly under the exclusive making-available right, not through the collecting body. Untested until the first tariffs. CE

Will Saudi recordings be paid abroad, and foreign recordings here?

Article 2(2) treats nationals of any treaty the Kingdom has joined as Saudi nationals, and the Royal Decree applies reciprocity. Saudi Arabia is in Berne and the Phonograms Convention but not Rome or WPPT, so reciprocity, not treaty membership alone, will decide. CE

Do we need to register with SAIP?

Registration is documentary and gives a rebuttable presumption of ownership. Where it opens for recordings, attach each holder's percentage (Arts. 45, 49). C

How much will collection cost?

The draft caps admin deductions at 25% of collections. For comparison, Greece's GEA ran at 22.4% and South Africa's SAMPRA at 19.9% in 2024. C

How to read it

  • C confirmed: stated in the instrument or a named official, legal or published source.
  • CE calibrated estimate: calculated or inferred, with the working shown.
  • D directional: a single secondary source, or not yet verified.
  • Orange is Saudi Arabia. Violet is the UAE and other comparison markets.

Methodology

  • Primary text: the Royal Decree and the gazetted Implementing Regulations.
  • Draft CMO regulation as reported in Arabic press; law-firm analysis from Gowling WLG, Baker McKenzie, Al Tamimi.
  • Market estimate: UAE tariff bands applied to MBC's broadcasting revenue and the licensed hotel count. Comparable bodies' collections per person × Saudi population as a cross-check.

Honesty

  • No estimate is presented as a fact.
  • The market estimate is the level once licensing is in place at UAE-style rates, not a year-one forecast.
  • No streaming data is used to size public performance.
  • No CMO data. Independent of any collecting body.

Limitations

  • Turkey's figure is producer distributions only; Morocco publishes none.
  • Portugal's figure may cover producers only.
  • Draft CMO regulation status after late August not verified.
  • UAE rates are single-source (Al Tamimi).
  • Term of protection unconfirmed.
